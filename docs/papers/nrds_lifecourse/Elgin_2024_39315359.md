## 摘要
<h4>Background</h4>Data on clinical outcomes of infants with birthweights less than 501 g (ELBW<sup><501</sup>) are limited.<h4>Objective</h4>To evaluate management strategies and clinical outcomes of ELBW<sup><501</sup>infants compared to infants weighing 501-750 g (ELBW<sup>501-750</sup>).<h4>Methods</h4>A retrospective study of all ELBW<sup><501</sup> and ELBW<sup>501-750</sup> infants born between 2012 and 2022 at a center utilizing first intention high frequency jet ventilation was performed. Patient characteristics, clinical and outcome data were compared between the two groups.<h4>Results</h4>A total of 358 infants (92 ELBW<sup><501</sup> infants and 266 ELBW<sup>501-750</sup>) were included. The survival rate for the ELBW<sup><501</sup> group was 60.9% compared to 86.5% for ELBW<sup>501-750</sup>. ELBW<sup><501</sup> infants required more frequent use of 2.0 mm endotracheal tubes, required higher FiO<sub>2</sub> and longer duration of mechanical ventilation. Compared to ELBW<sup>501-750</sup> group, the ELBW<sup><501</sup> group were more likely to be SGA (68.2% vs. 16.5%) and more premature (23.2 vs. 24.3 weeks) with lower survival, longer length of stay, higher incidence of ROP and lower weight at discharge but comparable rates of IVH, grade 3 BPD, discharged on supplemental oxygen, and tracheostomy.<h4>Conclusion</h4>ELBW<sup><501</sup> infants are at risk for significant morbidity and mortality. However, with specialized obstetric and neonatal care, survival rates of 60% are possible with respiratory outcomes comparable to ELBW<sup>501-750</sup> infants. However, the increased risk of severe ROP for ELBW<sup><501</sup> requiring either surgical or medical intervention is concerning and warrants optimal surveillance.
