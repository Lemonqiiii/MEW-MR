## 摘要
<h4>Background</h4>Extremely low gestational age newborns (ELGANs) are at risk of neurodevelopmental impairments that may originate in early NICU care. We hypothesized that early oxygen saturations (SpO<sub>2</sub>), arterial pO<sub>2</sub> levels, and supplemental oxygen (FiO<sub>2</sub>) would associate with later neuroanatomic changes.<h4>Methods</h4>SpO<sub>2</sub>, arterial blood gases, and FiO<sub>2</sub> from 73 ELGANs (GA 26.4 ± 1.2; BW 867 ± 179 g) during the first 3 postnatal days were correlated with later white matter injury (WM, MRI, n = 69), secondary cortical somatosensory processing in magnetoencephalography (MEG-SII, n = 39), Hempel neurological examination (n = 66), and developmental quotients of Griffiths Mental Developmental Scales (GMDS, n = 58).<h4>Results</h4>The ELGANs with later WM abnormalities exhibited lower SpO<sub>2</sub> and pO<sub>2</sub> levels, and higher FiO<sub>2</sub> need during the first 3 days than those with normal WM. They also had higher pCO<sub>2</sub> values. The infants with abnormal MEG-SII showed opposite findings, i.e., displayed higher SpO<sub>2</sub> and pO<sub>2</sub> levels and lower FiO<sub>2</sub> need, than those with better outcomes. Severe WM changes and abnormal MEG-SII were correlated with adverse neurodevelopment.<h4>Conclusions</h4>Low oxygen levels and high FiO<sub>2</sub> need during the NICU care associate with WM abnormalities, whereas higher oxygen levels correlate with abnormal MEG-SII. The results may indicate certain brain structures being more vulnerable to hypoxia and others to hyperoxia, thus emphasizing the role of strict saturation targets.<h4>Impact</h4>This study indicates that both abnormally low and high oxygen levels during early NICU care are harmful for later neurodevelopmental outcomes in preterm neonates.
