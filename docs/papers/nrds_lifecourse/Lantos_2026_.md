## 摘要
<title>Abstract</title>  <p>  <bold>Purpose:</bold>  In a prospective observational study we investigated whether a quality improvement program using systematic computerized data collection and analysis enables the safe introduction of advanced ventilation modes—Volume Guarantee (VG) and High Frequency Oscillatory Ventilation (HFOV)—into routine clinical care during emergency neonatal transport.  <bold>Methods:</bold>  Data were downloaded from ventilators during interhospital transport over an 8.5-year period. Ventilator parameters, waveforms and loops were reconstructed and discussed during case review meetings (n=21). Quality of respiratory care was assessed against a set of quality indicators for each year. Trends in ventilation practice were also analysed.  <bold>Results:</bold>  1,031 infants were included. The use of ventilation modes supporting all breaths increased from 38% in 2017-2020 to 56% in 2021-25 (p<0.001). VG use increased during the first two years (p<0.001) and remained stable between 82-87% subsequently. HFOV use increased from 2.6% in 2020 to 20.6% by 2025. pCO2 after the transport met criteria in 87% of the cases, with no significant change over the years. In infants ventilated without VG, the mean expired tidal volume was 4-6 mL/kg only in 31% (52/166) of cases. Significantly (p<0.001) higher proportion of infants ventilated with VG were in this range (493/758, 65%). Ventilator alarms were active in 5.8% of the time.  <bold>Conclusion:</bold>  Advanced ventilation modes can be safely introduced into clinical use when accompanied by systematic data collection and analysis. Regular case reviews lead to practice development without an explicit ventilation protocol.  </p>
