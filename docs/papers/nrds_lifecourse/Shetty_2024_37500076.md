## 摘要
<h4>Objective</h4>This study aimed to compare outcomes of infants who received less invasive surfactant administration (LISA) in the delivery suite (LISA-DS) with those who received LISA on the neonatal unit (LISA-NNU).<h4>Study design</h4>A prospective cohort study was undertaken of all infants who received LISA in a single center. Clinical outcomes included admission temperature, the need for intubation, durations of invasive and noninvasive ventilation, length of hospital stay and the incidences of bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), and requirement for home oxygen were compared between the two groups as were complications of the procedure.<h4>Results</h4>The 54 LISA-DS infants had similar gestational ages and birth weights to the 26 LISA-NNU infants (<i>p</i> = 0.732, 0.928, respectively). There were no significant differences between the admission temperatures (median [range]: 36.8 [36-38.7] vs. 36.8°C [36.4-37.7]; <i>p</i> = 0.451) or need for intubation in less than 72 hours of birth (28 vs. 23%, <i>p</i> = 0.656). The durations of invasive ventilation (median: 2 [0-65] vs. 1 [0-35] days; <i>p</i> = 0.188) and noninvasive ventilation (median: 37 [24-81] vs. 37 [3-225] days; <i>p</i> = 0.188) and the incidences of BPD (<i>p</i> = 0.818), IVH (<i>p</i> = 0.106), ROP (<i>p</i> = 0.526), and home oxygen requirement (<i>p</i> = 0.764) were similar.
