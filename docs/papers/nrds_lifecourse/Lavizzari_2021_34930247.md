## 摘要
<h4>Background</h4>Current criteria for surfactant administration assume that hypoxia is a direct marker of lung-volume de-recruitment. We first introduced an early, non-invasive assessment of lung mechanics by the Forced Oscillation Technique (FOT) and evaluated its role in predicting the need for surfactant therapy.<h4>Objectives</h4>To evaluate whether lung reactance (Xrs) assessment by FOT within 2 h of birth identifies infants who would need surfactant within 24 h; to eventually determine Xrs performance and a cut-off value for early detection of infants requiring surfactant.<h4>Methods</h4>We conducted a prospective, observational, non-randomized study in our tertiary NICU in Milan. Eligible infants were born between 27<sup>+0</sup> and 34<sup>+6</sup> weeks' gestation, presenting respiratory distress after birth.<h4>Exclusion criteria</h4>endotracheal intubation at birth, major malformations participation in other interventional trials, parental consent denied. We assessed Xrs during nasal CPAP at 5 cmH<sub>2</sub>O at 10 Hz within 2 h of life, recording flow and pressure tracing through a Fabian Ventilator for off-line analysis. Clinicians were blinded to FOT results.<h4>Results</h4>We enrolled 61 infants, with a median [IQR] gestational age of 31.9 [30.3; 32.9] weeks and birth weight 1490 [1230; 1816] g; 2 infants were excluded from the analysis for set-up malfunctioning. 14/59 infants received surfactant within 24 h. Xrs predicted surfactant need with a cut-off - 33.4 cmH<sub>2</sub>O*s/L and AUC-ROC = 0.86 (0.76-0.96), with sensitivity 0.85 and specificity 0.83.
