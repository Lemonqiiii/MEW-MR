## ⚠️ Asymmetry Analysis

### SEVERE_ASYMMETRY (Gap grade differs ≥2 levels)

#### ACS (G1) vs LISA (G4)
> **Quantification**: ACS has follow-up data on over 1.2 million children followed to age 5. LISA has ZERO published long-term follow-up data of any kind.
> **Clinical Implication**: Clinicians choosing between antenatal strategies (ACS) and postnatal strategies (LISA) are operating with fundamentally different levels of certainty about long-term consequences. The asymmetry is particularly concerning because LISA is being adopted across European NICUs at an accelerating pace — without even the most basic safety data beyond the neonatal period.
> **Paradox**: LISA is now A1-recommended (strongest) in the 2025 EU Guidelines, yet its evidence base for long-term outcomes is G4 (null). This juxtaposition of the highest recommendation level with the lowest evidence grade is unique among the interventions reviewed.

#### Caffeine (G1) vs LISA (G4)
> **Quantification**: Caffeine has 11-year cognitive and pulmonary follow-up from the CAP trial — the gold standard for neonatal long-term follow-up. LISA has zero.
> **Clinical Implication**: Two interventions commonly co-administered (caffeine facilitates spontaneous breathing needed for LISA) have polar-opposite evidence maturity. The absence of LISA follow-up data means the long-term safety of the caffeine+LISA combination — now standard care across Europe — is being determined in real time, on real patients, without systematic data collection.

#### ACS (G1) vs Diuretics (G4)
> **Quantification**: ACS evidence spans 30+ RCTs with meta-analysis. Diuretics have been used in BPD infants for decades with zero long-term renal/electrolyte/auditory follow-up data.
> **Clinical Implication**: This is arguably the most concerning asymmetry in neonatal pharmacotherapy: one of the most commonly prescribed drug classes in the NICU (diuretics) has no evidence base for long-term safety, while another (ACS) has a mature evidence base spanning 50 years.

## Top 5 Most Clinically Urgent Comparisons

1. **LISA vs INSURE — long-term safety**: LISA is A1-recommended with G4 evidence; INSURE is being displaced with marginally better follow-up. This is the most urgent comparison in neonatal respiratory care.
2. **Caffeine+LISA vs CPAP alone — interactive neurodevelopment**: The JAMA 2026 Dorner trial provides the first signal (23% vs 33% death/NDI, P=0.22, fine motor benefit P=0.03). Larger trials urgently needed.
3. **VTV+Late Dex vs VTV alone — combined BPD prevention**: No study has tested this as a bundled strategy despite both being independently recommended.
4. **Diuretics vs placebo — long-term renal safety**: Widely used, zero data. N-of-thousands natural experiment happening without data collection.
5. **Oxygen 90-94% vs individualized targets — lifespan outcomes**: NeOProM is the best evidence in neonatology, but still stops at age 5.
