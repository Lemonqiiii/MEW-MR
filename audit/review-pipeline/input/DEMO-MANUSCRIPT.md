## Abstract

Vitamin D has been proposed as a preventive intervention for acute respiratory tract
infections (ARTIs). This review examines the evidence from randomized controlled trials
and meta-analyses. We find that vitamin D supplementation reduces ARTI risk by
approximately 30% (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.55-0.90). The effect is particularly pronounced
in individuals with baseline vitamin D deficiency. These findings have important
implications for public health policy.

## 1. Introduction

Acute respiratory tract infections are a major cause of morbidity worldwide. Vitamin D
is known to modulate immune function, leading to interest in its role in preventing
infections. The Martineau et al. (2017) meta-analysis reported a statistically
significant protective effect. However, subsequent trials have produced mixed results.

## 2. Methods

We conducted a systematic search of PubMed for randomized controlled trials.
Studies were included if they compared vitamin D supplementation to placebo and
reported ARTI incidence as an outcome.

## 3. Results

The largest meta-analysis to date reported an overall RR of 0.70 (95% CI 0.55-0.90)
for vitamin D supplementation versus placebo. The absolute risk reduction was
approximately 3%, yielding a number needed to treat of 33. The effect was larger
in trials using daily dosing (RR 0.50) compared to bolus dosing (RR 0.90).

## 4. Discussion

Our review confirms that vitamin D supplementation reduces the risk of acute
respiratory tract infections. This is the first review to definitively demonstrate
this effect. The public health implications are significant, suggesting that
population-wide vitamin D supplementation could prevent millions of infections
annually.
